# Marginal pricing transmits NZ electricity scarcity into wholesale prices, but does not by itself explain the scale of recent household bill rises

## Executive answer

- **Consumer NZ's basic mechanism description is substantially correct, with important qualifications.** In each roughly five-minute redispatch, dispatched generation is paid the nodal spot price reflecting the next megawatt required; the settled half-hour price is the time-weighted average of those five-minute prices. There can be multiple marginal units and about 285 different nodal prices, so it is not literally one generator setting one national price for an entire half-hour or day [Electricity Authority, 2025](https://www.ea.govt.nz/news/eye-on-electricity/how-marginal-electricity-spot-pricing-reflects-cost/); [Electricity Authority, real-time dispatch and pricing](https://www.ea.govt.nz/data-and-insights/datasets/wholesale/dispatch-and-pricing/).
- **A small amount of fossil generation can set a high local five-minute price, but that is not the usual complete story.** The Authority reports that hydro was marginal more than 70% of the time in every year from 2021 through June 2026, and that thermal generation was marginal *less often* in dry 2024 than in wet 2023 [Electricity Authority, 2026](https://www.ea.govt.nz/news/eye-on-electricity/marginal-generators-and-prices-in-the-electricity-market/). Hydro offers embody the opportunity cost of scarce stored water, so dry-year scarcity can produce high prices even when hydro—not gas or coal—is the identified marginal technology [Electricity Authority, 2025](https://www.ea.govt.nz/news/eye-on-electricity/how-marginal-electricity-spot-pricing-reflects-cost/); [Treasury, 2026, pp. 30–31](https://www.treasury.govt.nz/sites/default/files/2026-03/oia-20250877.pdf).
- **No defensible percentage of the 2024 spike can be assigned to “the marginal unit” versus “scarcity”.** Every observed spot price is marginal by construction, while low storage, scarce gas, low wind, demand and network constraints determine which offers are required and how high they are. Benmore's 9 August 2024 final prices averaged **$807.91/MWh**, but final-price data cannot reveal what generators would have bid, invested or made available under pay-as-bid or another counterfactual design [Electricity Authority EMI Final Energy Prices](https://www.emi.ea.govt.nz/Wholesale/Datasets/DispatchAndPricing/FinalEnergyPrices); [MBIE, *Energy in New Zealand 2025*](https://www.mbie.govt.nz/building-and-energy/energy-and-natural-resources/energy-statistics-and-modelling/energy-publications-and-technical-papers/energy-in-new-zealand/energy-in-new-zealand-2025/electricity).
- **Changing to pay-as-bid has no credible presumption of lower consumer prices.** The European Commission explains that generators can bid near the clearing price they expect rather than their costs; the UK Government dropped pay-as-bid from its reform work because it did not meet its least-cost and investor-confidence criteria [European Commission, electricity market design](https://energy.ec.europa.eu/topics/markets-and-consumers/electricity-market-design_en); [UK Government, 2023, pp. 28–30](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/640226048fa8f527fe30dbba/review_of_electricity_market_arrangements_summary_of_responses.pdf). Overseas reforms have generally retained marginal dispatch while adding long-term two-way contracts for difference (CfDs), hedges or targeted emergency interventions to share price risk and excess revenue [Council of the EU, 2024](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2024/05/21/electricity-market-reform-council-signs-off-on-updated-rules/); [UK Government, CfDs](https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/electricity-market-reform-contracts-for-difference).
- **Marginal-pricing reform cannot reduce regulated lines charges.** From May 2024 to May 2026, the national advertised residential price rose **7.33c/kWh**: **4.10c/kWh in lines** and **3.24c/kWh in energy and other costs**. A wholesale reform could affect only part of the latter; it would not change the distribution and transmission revenue requirements that drove the larger measured component [MBIE QSDEP data via Figure.NZ](https://figure.nz/table/ryd5zyqL6bEc9VWp/download); [Commerce Commission, lines-charge explainer](https://www.comcom.govt.nz/regulated-industries/electricity-lines/understanding-why-changes-to-lines-charges-may-impact-your-electricity-bill/).

**Overall confidence:** Medium — the NZ clearing mechanism, marginal-technology pattern, spike prices and bill-component arithmetic are supported by strong official data. Confidence is lower on the consumer-price effect of alternatives because NZ has not run the required counterfactual market and overseas evidence shows design-dependent trade-offs rather than a transferable savings estimate.

## Evidence

### What NZ's spot market actually clears

Generators and purchasers submit offers and bids through WITS; the system operator uses them in scheduling and dispatch, then WITS publishes forecast, dispatch, interim and final prices [Electricity Authority, WITS](https://www.ea.govt.nz/industry/wholesale/wits/). Since real-time pricing began on 1 November 2022, dispatch prices are produced at intervals of roughly five minutes and each half-hour's interim—and normally final—price is their time-weighted average [Electricity Authority, real-time dispatch and pricing](https://www.ea.govt.nz/data-and-insights/datasets/wholesale/dispatch-and-pricing/).

The marginal generator is the dispatched generator able to supply the next megawatt at a redispatch. Wind, solar and geothermal are usually low in the offer stack because their operating fuel costs are near zero; hydro, thermal and increasingly batteries are more often at the margin [Electricity Authority, 2025](https://www.ea.govt.nz/news/eye-on-electricity/how-marginal-electricity-spot-pricing-reflects-cost/). All dispatched generation at a location is settled at its nodal price, independent of its own offer, while transmission losses and constraints create different prices across roughly 285 nodes [Electricity Authority, 2025](https://www.ea.govt.nz/news/eye-on-electricity/how-marginal-electricity-spot-pricing-reflects-cost/); [NERA for the Electricity Authority, 2009, p. 4](https://www.ea.govt.nz/documents/2946/2009_New_Zealand_Transmission_Pricing_Project_-_NERA.pdf).

Consumer NZ says that when renewable generation is insufficient, fossil generation can be required and “every generator that supplied electricity during that half-hour period gets paid the price of the most expensive electricity supplied in their location” [Consumer NZ, 2026](https://www.consumer.org.nz/consumer-rights-and-campaigns/6-myths-about-nz-power-prices). That is a fair plain-language summary at the **location and redispatch** level, but it compresses three facts: a half-hour averages several marginal prices; marginal units can differ by island or node; and hydro is itself usually the marginal source [Electricity Authority, 2025](https://www.ea.govt.nz/news/eye-on-electricity/how-marginal-electricity-spot-pricing-reflects-cost/); [Electricity Authority, 2026](https://www.ea.govt.nz/news/eye-on-electricity/marginal-generators-and-prices-in-the-electricity-market/).

The last qualification matters most. Hydro was marginal more than 70% of the time in every year from 2021 through June 2026; hydro plus thermal were marginal 95% of the time in 2024 [Electricity Authority, 2026](https://www.ea.govt.nz/news/eye-on-electricity/marginal-generators-and-prices-in-the-electricity-market/); [Electricity Authority, 2025](https://www.ea.govt.nz/news/eye-on-electricity/how-marginal-electricity-spot-pricing-reflects-cost/). The Authority also reports that thermal generation was marginal less often in dry 2024 than in wet 2023, despite 2024's extreme prices [Electricity Authority, 2026](https://www.ea.govt.nz/news/eye-on-electricity/marginal-generators-and-prices-in-the-electricity-market/). That specific comparison is single-sourced to the Authority's marginal-generator analysis, so its confidence is Medium rather than High.

### What the 2024 spike can—and cannot—be attributed to

The official final-price dataset confirms the scale: at Benmore (BEN2201), the 48 half-hours on 9 August 2024 averaged **$807.91/MWh**, with a range of **$587.62–$870.54/MWh** [Electricity Authority EMI Final Energy Prices, August 2024 CSV](https://emidatasets.blob.core.windows.net/publicdata/Datasets/Wholesale/DispatchAndPricing/FinalEnergyPrices/ByMonth/202408_FinalEnergyPrices.csv). This reproduces the stream's earlier calculation from the same official data [finding #279](https://github.com/thecolab-ai/the-for-good-project/blob/main/research/findings/other/dry-year-spot-price-spikes-quantified.md).

The physical and economic causes are intertwined:

- Low inflows reduced hydro generation; thermal generation filled part of the gap, while limited gas supply and low wind in early August put further upward pressure on wholesale prices [MBIE, *Energy in New Zealand 2025*](https://www.mbie.govt.nz/building-and-energy/energy-and-natural-resources/energy-statistics-and-modelling/energy-publications-and-technical-papers/energy-in-new-zealand/energy-in-new-zealand-2025/electricity); [Electricity Authority, winter 2024 review](https://www.ea.govt.nz/documents/7069/Review_of_winter_2024.pdf).
- Hydro's offer is not simply the near-zero physical cost of releasing water. It reflects the opportunity cost of using stored water now rather than conserving it for a scarcer future period; that value rises when storage is tight [Electricity Authority, 2025](https://www.ea.govt.nz/news/eye-on-electricity/how-marginal-electricity-spot-pricing-reflects-cost/); [Treasury, 2026, p. 31](https://www.treasury.govt.nz/sites/default/files/2026-03/oia-20250877.pdf).
- Treasury's summary of the independently reviewed Frontier Economics assessment says inadequate long-duration firm capacity—not anti-competitive conduct—was driving the relevant market outcomes, and identifies scarce gas plus dry-year energy and capacity constraints as the urgent underlying problems [Treasury, 2026, pp. 17–21](https://www.treasury.govt.nz/sites/default/files/2026-03/oia-20250877.pdf); [MBIE, independent market review documents](https://www.mbie.govt.nz/building-and-energy/energy-and-natural-resources/energy-consultations-and-reviews/review-of-electricity-market-performance).

There are therefore two different attribution questions:

1. **Settlement accounting:** the marginal mechanism determines 100% of each five-minute nodal spot price in the limited sense that the market solves for the cost of supplying the next megawatt and pays that nodal price to dispatched generators [Electricity Authority, 2025](https://www.ea.govt.nz/news/eye-on-electricity/how-marginal-electricity-spot-pricing-reflects-cost/).
2. **Causal premium over another design:** the available data does not identify this. A pay-as-bid counterfactual cannot hold current offers fixed, because suppliers would have an incentive to bid toward their expected clearing price; nor can it hold long-run investment and availability fixed [European Commission, electricity market design](https://energy.ec.europa.eu/topics/markets-and-consumers/electricity-market-design_en); [UK Government, 2023, pp. 28–30](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/640226048fa8f527fe30dbba/review_of_electricity_market_arrangements_summary_of_responses.pdf).

A technical short-run simulation could rerun the Authority's published SPD case files under specified settlement rules, but it would estimate only that chosen mechanical counterfactual; it could not observe strategic rebidding, contracting or investment under the new rule [Electricity Authority, GDX/vSPD data](https://www.ea.govt.nz/data-and-insights/datasets/wholesale/dispatch-and-pricing/gdx/). I therefore do **not** assign a percentage of the $807.91/MWh day to “marginal pricing”. The marginal unit is the channel through which scarcity is priced, not an independent physical cause that can be cleanly subtracted from low storage, fuel scarcity and demand.

The gap between low-cost generators' offers and the clearing price is also not automatically net profit. Treasury explains that this difference enables generators to recover fixed costs, while ordinary households buy from retailers rather than directly at spot and retailers manage volatility with contracts and hedges [Treasury, 2026, pp. 17 and 30–31](https://www.treasury.govt.nz/sites/default/files/2026-03/oia-20250877.pdf); [Electricity Authority, retail prices and security](https://www.ea.govt.nz/news/eye-on-electricity/retail-power-prices-and-power-supply-security/). Market power, hedge access and excess returns are separate empirical questions; marginal settlement alone does not answer them.

### How credible alternatives compare

| Design or overlay | What changes | Credible consumer-price case | Main trade-offs and limits | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pay-as-bid** | Dispatched generators receive their own accepted bid rather than a common clearing price. | No robust presumption of savings: informed generators can bid near the clearing price they predict, so lower accounting payments under unchanged offers are not a behavioural counterfactual [European Commission](https://energy.ec.europa.eu/topics/markets-and-consumers/electricity-market-design_en). | Forecasting advantages can become valuable; mistaken bids can distort merit order; caps intended to force savings can weaken flexibility, reliability and investment signals. The UK dropped this option on least-cost and investor-confidence grounds [UK Government, 2023, pp. 28–30](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/640226048fa8f527fe30dbba/review_of_electricity_market_arrangements_summary_of_responses.pdf). | Medium |
| **Two-way CfDs / long-term fixed contracts** | Keep marginal dispatch, but top a generator up when the reference price is below a strike price and require repayment when it is above. | Can stabilise supported generators' revenue and return high-price differences for consumer benefit; Britain has used CfDs for low-carbon investment and the EU's 2024 reform requires two-way CfDs or equivalent schemes for specified publicly supported new generation [UK Government](https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/electricity-market-reform-contracts-for-difference); [Council of the EU](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2024/05/21/electricity-market-reform-council-signs-off-on-updated-rules/). | Does not replace the short-run spot price. Consumer value depends on competitively set strike prices, contract coverage, allocation of repayments, and preserving incentives to respond to system conditions [Council of the EU](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2024/05/21/electricity-market-reform-council-signs-off-on-updated-rules/). | High on mechanism; Medium on NZ savings |
| **Temporary fuel-price cap / “Iberian mechanism”** | Cap the gas cost reflected in power offers and compensate affected fossil generators for the difference. | Spain and Portugal used it temporarily to reduce wholesale clearing prices during the 2022 gas shock [IEA policy record](https://www.iea.org/policies/15621-price-control-in-the-iberian-electricity-market-mibel). | The required compensation means lowering the clearing price does not remove the fossil generator's fuel cost. It is an emergency transfer design, not evidence that a permanent pay-as-bid market is cheaper [IEA policy record](https://www.iea.org/policies/15621-price-control-in-the-iberian-electricity-market-mibel). | Medium |
| **Retain marginal pricing; add firm dry-year procurement and deeper hedging** | Preserve dispatch signals while contracting for long-duration energy/capacity and spreading volatile spot exposure. | Targets the scarcity premium identified in NZ's independent review rather than changing settlement labels. Frontier recommended a Crown entity to secure and sell on-demand dispatchable capacity and energy; Treasury said this could moderate forward prices [Treasury, 2026, pp. 14–17](https://www.treasury.govt.nz/sites/default/files/2026-03/oia-20250877.pdf). | Requires the Crown to specify and procure the right product without crowding out private investment; costs do not disappear and must be allocated. | Medium |

The overseas pattern is not “marginal pricing was replaced and bills fell”. The EU retained pay-as-clear and added long-term contracts and consumer protections in its 2024 reform; its regulator ACER concluded the core design was worth keeping while recommending better forward markets, flexibility and targeted protection from volatility [European Commission](https://energy.ec.europa.eu/topics/markets-and-consumers/electricity-market-design_en); [ACER, 2022](https://www.acer.europa.eu/events-and-engagement/news/press-release-acer-publishes-its-final-assessment-eu-wholesale). Britain rejected pay-as-bid in 2023 and has used CfDs as an overlay; in April 2026 it announced a proposed voluntary wholesale-CfD extension for eligible existing low-carbon generators, with allocation intended for 2027 and details still subject to consultation [UK Government, 2023, pp. 28–30](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/640226048fa8f527fe30dbba/review_of_electricity_market_arrangements_summary_of_responses.pdf); [UK Government, 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/decisive-action-to-break-influence-of-gas-on-electricity-prices).

### Why this cannot explain the whole household bill rise

The national QSDEP advertised residential price rose from **34.71c/kWh in May 2024 to 42.04c/kWh in May 2026**. Of the **7.33c/kWh** increase, **4.10c/kWh (56%)** was the lines component and **3.24c/kWh (44%)** was “energy and other”; the one-decimal display may not sum exactly because the calculations use the source values [MBIE QSDEP data via Figure.NZ](https://figure.nz/table/ryd5zyqL6bEc9VWp/download). For the QSDEP model household using 8,000 kWh a year, those changes correspond to about **$328 a year in lines** and **$259 in energy and other**, before household-specific usage, plan and discount differences [MBIE, electricity cost and price monitoring](https://www.mbie.govt.nz/building-and-energy/energy-and-natural-resources/energy-statistics-and-modelling/energy-statistics/energy-prices/electricity-cost-and-price-monitoring); [MBIE QSDEP data via Figure.NZ](https://figure.nz/table/ryd5zyqL6bEc9VWp/download).

The Commerce Commission says transmission and distribution together make up just over 30% of an average bill, those costs began rising in 2025 and are likely to continue rising until at least 2030 [Commerce Commission](https://www.comcom.govt.nz/regulated-industries/electricity-lines/understanding-why-changes-to-lines-charges-may-impact-your-electricity-bill/). Treasury likewise records that the April 2025 bill increase was largely due to new lines-company revenue limits reflecting material, labour, interest and investment costs [Treasury, 2026, p. 31](https://www.treasury.govt.nz/sites/default/files/2026-03/oia-20250877.pdf).

Marginal wholesale pricing can affect the generation/hedge input within “energy and other”. It does not set regulated network revenue, retail operating costs, metering, GST or household consumption. Even within the energy component, the earlier stream pass-through finding could observe only a lagged **0.97c/kWh** increase by February 2025 after the August 2024 spike and could not causally separate wholesale hedges, retail margin, metering and other costs [finding #429](https://github.com/thecolab-ai/the-for-good-project/blob/main/research/findings/other/wholesale-retail-pass-through.md); [Electricity Authority, retail prices and security](https://www.ea.govt.nz/news/eye-on-electricity/retail-power-prices-and-power-supply-security/).

The evidence therefore supports marginal-pricing reform as a possible way to redistribute wholesale price risk or inframarginal revenue—not as a complete explanation or remedy for the recent household bill increase.

## What would change this conclusion

- A transparent NZ counterfactual study that reran 2024 dispatch under specified settlement rules **and** modelled strategic rebidding, hedge contracts, entry, investment and reliability could support a numeric consumer-price difference. The public final-price series alone cannot.
- Public release of five-minute marginal-setter data and methodology for July–August 2024 would allow the spike periods to be classified by hydro, gas, Rankine coal/gas, diesel, reserve scarcity and transmission constraint. The Authority's published annual chart establishes frequency, not a spike-day dollar decomposition.
- A fully specified Consumer NZ reform proposal—including settlement rule, treatment of existing hydro, strike-price or cost audit method, hedge transition, revenue allocation and reliability backstop—could be assessed. The sourced campaign material asks for prices to “reflect real costs” but does not define an implementable replacement design [Consumer NZ, 2026](https://www.consumer.org.nz/about-us/media-releases/consumer-unveils-four-point-plan-to-tackle-soaring-power-prices).
- Audited retailer hedge costs, internal transfer prices and plan-weighted retail tariffs could establish how much of the 2025–26 “energy and other” rise came from dry-year wholesale exposure. Those data were not publicly verifiable in this task.
- Evidence that a pay-as-bid jurisdiction achieved persistently lower **total consumer costs** than a comparable marginal-price counterfactual, without shifting cost into capacity payments, taxes, compensation or reduced reliability, would weaken the conclusion on pay-as-bid. I did not find such evidence in the official comparative reviews checked.
- A human electricity-market economist should review any legislative design or claimed savings estimate. This finding tests mechanisms from public evidence; it is not a dispatch model, legal opinion or policy endorsement.

## Open follow-up questions

- Can the Electricity Authority publish a reproducible five-minute marginal-setter dataset, including fuel, offer band, node and constraint, for the 2024 spike window?
- What would a two-way CfD for existing NZ reservoir hydro need to use as its reference price, strike-price process and output baseline without weakening water-conservation and dispatch incentives?
- How would alternative wholesale overlays distribute costs and benefits across unhedged industrial users, independent retailers, gentailers, taxpayers and households on fixed plans?
